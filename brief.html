Proof-of-Solvency for Tokenized Private Credit

A fund proves it is solvent and fully KYC'd, over a book it cannot fake, without revealing a single loan.

Product name TBD. A design-partner brief.

The problem

Private credit is the largest tokenized real-world-asset category, roughly $20.5B on-chain today, on top of a $1.8 to $3.14 trillion off-chain private credit market. But the thing that backs the token, the loan book, lives off-chain in servicer systems and spreadsheets.

So the hardest question an investor, counterparty, or regulator can ask is also the one nobody answers well: prove this token is actually over-collateralised right now, without showing me the private loan book. Today the answer is a periodic manual attestation or a trust-me dashboard. Smart-contract audits do not help. They verify the code, not the asset state.

What we built

A way for a fund to prove, cryptographically and revealing nothing, that it is solvent and compliant before it is allowed to mint. A zero-knowledge proof attests, over a private loan book, that:

  • Solvent: performing collateral covers the required over-collateralisation of the token supply
  • Compliant: every borrower passed KYC
  • Authentic: the book hashes to a commitment the servicer signed

How the mint-gate works

  1. The servicer signs a commitment to the current loan book.
  2. A zero-knowledge proof shows the private book is solvent and fully KYC'd, and hashes to that commitment.
  3. The token mints only if both the proof and the servicer signature check out. A swapped or insolvent book is blocked, because a different book has a different commitment the servicer never signed.

What we do not solve, and where you come in

A proof certifies that the attested data is solvent. It does not certify the data is true. The security rests on the data-trust anchor: the servicer or custodian signing the loan tape that feeds the proof. That integration, a signed and authenticated loan-tape feed from your servicing stack, is exactly what a design partner helps define against a real book.

The ask

One design partner

A tokenized-credit protocol or fund willing to feed a loan tape (test or anonymised is fine) and co-define the integration. In return:

  • Free integration and hands-on engineering through the pilot
  • The reference deployment, and a hand in shaping the standard for verifiable tokenized-credit solvency
  • A continuous, privacy-preserving proof your LPs, counterparties, and regulators can check, instead of a quarterly PDF

If you tokenize private credit and "prove you are solvent without showing the book" is a real problem for you, that is the conversation.
